## Prose · Where this chapter's claims come from

Step 1 reads the gate. Speaker labels exist only in full meeting mode (`Voxt/internal/meeting/mode.go:17-19`), and the diarizer is asked for only when MTD is installed and the meeting's language matches the two MTD knows, zh and en (`Voxt/meeting_asr.go:33-77`; the language list is `Voxt/internal/omni/models.go:267-268`). A missing model or another language means a plain transcript, exactly Chapter 13's. Only the system track is diarized (`Voxt/internal/meeting/finalize.go:147-156`); a mic line with no speaker is stored as `me` (`finalize.go:396-401`).

Step 2 reads the hold release. The final pass reads tracks in order and, before the system track's diarize, releases the selected speech model's hold: MTD cannot start until that hold lets its server stop (`finalize.go:40-56`).

Step 3 reads the chunking. One MTD request covers at most two minutes of a track (`diarizeChunkMax`, `finalize.go:175-178`), small enough that a dictation waits at most one request and far from the native decoder's own 20-minute split. With the pause rule set to the same two minutes, cuts land at the cap, and a hard cut restarts one second early (`finalize.go:189-196`; `Voxt/internal/meeting/chunks.go:26-38`). Every request carries MOSS's own instruction, which asks each segment to start with a timestamp and a speaker label ([S01], [S02], …) (`finalize.go:179-183`); a stored label drops the S and the leading zero, S02 → s2 (`finalize.go:282-297`).

Step 4 reads the carry. The native decoder starts its labels over per chunk, so every request after the first carries the last labeled lines with the note that the same voices keep their labels (`finalize.go:189-232`); the carry is trimmed to a 300-rune screen (`finalize.go:270-272`). A line the previous chunk already returned (ending at or before the covered point) is skipped (`finalize.go:233-240`).

Step 5 reads the anchor. A line the model repeated from the overlap — ending at or before the covered point, at least two words — whose words match an earlier labeled line pins this chunk's number for that voice to the earlier speaker (`anchorSpeakers`, `finalize.go:321-357`). A voice missing from the overlap keeps the number the model gave it, which is why the carry does the main work; a one-word line anchors nothing. The chapter draws the anchor folding one renumbered voice back; a chunk where the model renumbers several voices can only repair the ones the overlap proves.

Step 6 reads the public sample. `Voxt/docs/MTDEvaluation.zh-CN.md` (2026-11-16, Apple M5 Max) spliced synthetic voices (Qwen3-TTS) with 1.5 s of quiet into 2-, 3- and 4-speaker clips with a known layout: 11 of 11 segment labels correct, three identical rounds, text error 1.1–2.9% against 17.9% for the plain-text instruction. The document itself flags that the perfect score used the old scoring rule (each label attributed to its majority voice, last round only); the rule was tightened to best one-to-one matching over all three rounds and the eval has not been rerun. The voices are clean and take turns: an upper bound, with noise, overlapping speech and far-field untested. The code sets no four-person cap; five or more is unproven, not forbidden.

Constructed parts: the three-minute meeting, its lines (`let's review the budget`, `sure, starting with March`, `sounds right`, `I'll send the file`) and their timestamps are invented; the selected model drawn as Qwen3-ASR is one example of the held model. No real meeting, recognition model or diarizer was run. How the labeled transcript is corrected, summarized, asked about and exported is Chapter 15.
