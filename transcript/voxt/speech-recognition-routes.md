## Prose · Where this chapter's claims come from

Step 1 uses the public provider interfaces to map three alternatives: local Omni, a configured ASR API and macOS system dictation. Its one-second, 32,000-byte recording is a constructed example derived from the capture format in Chapter 3; only the selected service receives it. An API hosted at a loopback address also stays on this Mac. Step 2 uses the session's file-transcriber branch and an OpenAI-compatible WAV upload; its three-second recording and returned sentence are constructed, while the 44-byte WAV header and size formula come from public code. Step 3 follows the public stream protocol; three sends and their English draft texts are constructed to show that each partial replaces the whole current hypothesis. Step 4 uses the public `SplitPCM` test's ten-second fixture, nine-second limit and 300 ms pause at 8.5 seconds; its two-piece result and last-quarter, 100 ms search rule come from public code. Step 5 constructs two English results to demonstrate `TranscribeInPieces` and `JoinText`; ordered recognition, blank omission, failure behavior and script-dependent spacing follow public code. Step 6 applies the public Qwen3-ASR 100 ms first-decode and one-second interval settings to a constructed three-second example; with `LiveResult` off, the default Final behavior comes from the local Omni implementation. Step 7 follows final text into `Snapshot.Raw`; its sentence is constructed, while trimming, end-word removal and empty-result handling come from the public session code. Language-model cleanup is deliberately left to the next chapter.
