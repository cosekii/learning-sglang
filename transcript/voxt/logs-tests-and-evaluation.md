## Prose · Where this chapter's claims come from

Step 1 builds the map. The route's six stations reuse the names from Ch. 1 Step 2. The three tag kinds are this chapter's own legend: code tests (Step 2), fixed-sample evaluations (Step 3) and real-device checks (Step 4), with logs and diagnostics in Step 5 and the tagged map in Step 6. The question bar quotes Ch. 15 Step 6's closing question.

Step 2 counts the test files. At the pinned source tree, `Voxt/` holds 515 Go test files: 258 in the root package, 53 in `internal/session`, 36 in `internal/meeting`, 16 in `internal/asr`, 13 in `internal/omni`, 10 in `internal/llm`, 8 in `internal/compose` and 121 across the remaining packages (counted with `git ls-tree` on the pinned revision). The three pinned examples are real: the 9-second limit, 300 ms pause and 10-second fixture of Ch. 4 Step 4 come from the public `SplitPCM` test (`Voxt/internal/asr/split_test.go`); the supervisor tests run a fake helper through crashes, restarts, protocol mismatches and garble (`Voxt/internal/helper/supervisor_test.go`, the `VOXTNEW_FAKE_HELPER` path); the log package's tests cover rotation, drop warnings and the redaction path (`Voxt/internal/logs/logs_test.go`). One file can hold many checks; the count is of files.

Step 3 reads the evaluation set. The eval doc defines 29 short clips (169 s in all) and 2 long ones (95 s and 104 s), all synthetic Qwen3-TTS voices, and warns that real dictation scores worse (`Voxt/docs/eval.md`). The dictionary score is its context-bias result: of 10 dictated terms, 7 landed with the dictionary as context and 4 without. The multi-speaker 11/11 of Ch. 14 Step 6 was measured under the old scoring rule and never re-run (`Voxt/docs/MTDEvaluation.zh-CN.md`). The doc's large LLM sections are kept as a record: those evals ran the retired `voxtnew-llm` and were removed with it, so they no longer run.

Step 4 reads the manual checklist. The public manual-test doc is an about-80-minute real-Mac checklist for what automated tests cannot reach — permissions, the event tap, real Accessibility reads and the real microphone (`Voxt/docs/manual-test.md`). Its section 10 names the automated real-Mac scripts `mac-e2e.sh` and `mac-regress.sh`, which still run on a real Mac.

Step 5 reads the diary. The log service keeps the newest 2,000 lines in memory and writes `voxtnew.log` plus two rotated files, each capped at 5 MB (`Voxt/internal/logs/logs.go:28-33,64-70,224-230`). Keys, tokens and proxy passwords are redacted before any line reaches the panel, the files or a bundle (`logs.go:93-95`); speech content is off by default (a privacy switch turns it on). The diagnostics bundle zips `diagnostics.txt`, `settings.json` and the logs — no words the user said (`Voxt/settings_about_controller.go:61-92,174-216`) — and the prefilled bug issue truncates the diagnostics to 5,000 runes (`settings_about_controller.go:265-271`). The stage's three log lines are constructed; their format follows `logs.go:167`.

Step 6 tags the map. A tag means this book showed pinned evidence at that station, not a coverage audit: test files exist for every station's package (hotkey, helper, asr/omni, llm/compose, deliver, store/session), the sample evaluations cover recognition (Steps 3 and Ch. 14 Step 6), and the real-Mac list covers the human parts — the key listener, permissions, the microphone and real Accessibility reads (Step 4). The open band quotes the eval doc's own not-measured list (noise, real voices, accents, microphone recordings, sessions through the helper's real capture) and the multi-speaker doc's unproven ground (more than 4 speakers, real meeting audio).
