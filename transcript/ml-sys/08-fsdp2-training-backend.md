Step 1 contrasts full replication with sharding at `fsdp/readme.md:16`. The constructed model has `4,000,000,000` values, with 2-byte weights and 2-byte gradients. Its optimizer convention comes from `readme-2-en.md:21-31`: FP32 master weights (the full-precision update copy), momentum (a running gradient average) and variance (a running squared-gradient average), or `3 × 4 = 12` bytes per value. Thus one full copy uses `4B × (2 + 2 + 12) = 64 GB`, 80% of the shared 80 GB H100 capacity. Eight copies use 512 GB. These numbers describe only the three saved table kinds, not activations or temporary allocations.

Step 2 uses the same source's FSDP alternative: each rank owns a shard of the parameters, gradients and optimizer state (`fsdp/readme.md:16-23`; `readme-2-en.md:43-47`). The equal eight-way split gives `64 ÷ 8 = 8 GB` per rank and 64 GB in aggregate. FSDP2's per-parameter DTensor representation is explained in the deep dive. The DP mesh determines the shard count: slime computes `dp_size = world_size // cp_size` and passes `dp_mesh` to `fully_shard` (`actor.py:158-186,94-98` at the pinned revision). Eight physical GPUs imply DP 8 only when CP is 1, as in this example.

Step 3 follows `fsdp/readme.md:18`: forward all-gathers the parameters for the unit being computed, then releases the full copy. The unit is the layer or parameter group materialized together (`readme-2-en.md:101-105`). This is the initial forward after `optimizer.zero_grad(set_to_none=True)` (`actor.py:522-535` at the pinned revision), so the old gradient shard is absent. The starting residency is 1 GB of weights + 6 GB of optimizer-related tables = 7 GB, not the complete 8 GB training-state bill. The figure writes the temporary layer size as `U` because the sources provide no value. Backward restores the 1 GB gradient shard and the full 8 GB bill.

Step 4 follows `fsdp/readme.md:19` and `readme-2-en.md:47-58`. Each rank trains on different records. Immediately before backward, slime executes `loss = loss * dp_size / global_batch_size` (`actor.py:687-689` at the pinned revision), and its FSDP2 policy sets `reduce_dtype=torch.float32` (`actor.py:1000-1034`). On this FP32 path, FSDP2's default reduce-scatter uses `ReduceOp.AVG`, with no extra predivide or postdivide factor (`pytorch/pytorch@v2.8.0`, `_fsdp_collectives.py:605-641`). Each rank therefore retains the average of the pre-scaled gradients for its shard. AVG supplies `1/R`; together with slime's `R/B` pre-scale, the result is `1/B` times the sum of the unscaled rank gradients, the global-batch mean. The label shows the all-layers gradient total: 8 GB ÷ 8 = 1 GB per rank. This is only the gradient table's shard, not the rank's 8 GB total training state or a layer's temporary size. The optimizer updates the owned weight shards after all layers finish (`actor.py:701-702`). The deep dive verifies the two factors with a separate `R=2`, `B=8` scalar example.
