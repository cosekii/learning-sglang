## Prose · Where this chapter's claims come from

The 250 ms value is a constructed example from [zero-to-sglang Part I Chapter 5: Introduction to Benchmark](https://zero-to-sglang.datawhale.cc/eng/part1/Chapter5_Introduction_to_Benchmark.html), §3.1. The later token times are constructed additions to that example, not measurements from this computer. The TTFT and ITL definitions follow the same section and *Inference Engineering* §1.4: TTFT runs from sending a request until its first output token arrives, and ITL is the gap between neighboring output tokens. TPOT follows the course's post-first-token boundary: divide the ITL total by the number of gaps, not by all output tokens. E2E latency follows the course boundary from request sent to completion: subtract the endpoints, or add TTFT to all post-first-token gaps. The 100-request latency distribution is another constructed example, based on the course's 99 fast requests and one 2 s request in §5.2; P50 follows the middle-position guidance in §5.1 and *Inference Engineering* §1.4.1. P99 follows the same sources' guidance to inspect the slower requests; this chapter explicitly uses the rank method shown in Steps 5–6 rather than claiming a universal percentile algorithm. Throughput follows the course's definition in §3.2: completed requests divided by total test time. The 20-second whole-test window is a constructed addition, not a local measurement.
