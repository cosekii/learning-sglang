## Prose · Where this chapter's claims come from

Step 1 uses a constructed ten-second response whose reader has taken four seconds. Qwen3-TTS documents 24 kHz float32 audio at about 96 kB per second, so the six-second backlog is `24,000 × 4 × 6 = 576,000 B`. A finished request releases its generation-capacity slot, but streamed audio can remain in the API process until the client reads it (`docs/cookbook/qwen3_tts/tuning.md:22-26`; `sglang_omni/pipeline/coordinator.py:103-109,213-223`). Socket buffering is deliberately excluded from this teaching arithmetic.

Step 2 shows the shipped `stream_delivery.max_total_backlog_bytes` default: 1 GiB per coordinator/API process, with `none` disabling the cap (`sglang_omni/config/schema.py:641-648,793-796`; `docs/cookbook/qwen3_tts/tuning.md:24-28`). The accounting sums only top-level `bytes` values in a message payload. The constructed six-byte audio payload and 100-character string therefore contribute six bytes, not 106 (`sglang_omni/pipeline/coordinator.py:91-100`). Text, socket buffers, total process memory and other API processes sit outside this ledger.

Step 3 defines reader idleness from coordinator queue reads, not playback. `queue.get()` subtracts the delivered payload from both ledgers and sets `reader_idle_since_s` to the current monotonic time. A later produced chunk increases the backlog without changing that timestamp; when an empty backlog first becomes nonempty, the producer starts the idle clock (`sglang_omni/pipeline/coordinator.py:103-109,497-520,1088-1096`; `tests/unit_test/pipeline/test_coordinator.py:1599-1620`). The two-second timestamps are constructed.

Step 4 reproduces the unit test's 12 B example. `req-idle` holds 4 B with the older reader timestamp; `req-behind` holds 8 B with a fresher reader. Another 4 B for `req-behind` makes 16 B. The coordinator fails `req-idle`, subtracts its 4 B and leaves 12 B. Selection uses the oldest `reader_idle_since_s` among streams with positive backlog, not the largest backlog, and the cap triggers only while the total is strictly greater (`sglang_omni/pipeline/coordinator.py:1102-1131`; `tests/unit_test/pipeline/test_coordinator.py:1516-1539`). The tiny byte counts and adjusted timestamps are test values.

Step 5 reproduces the second 12 B unit test. The streams hold 2, 4 and 4 B; the fresh stream adds 6 B, making 16 B. Failing the oldest 2 B stream leaves 14 B, still over the cap. Failing the next-oldest 4 B stream leaves 10 B and increments `failed_stream_count` to two (`sglang_omni/pipeline/coordinator.py:1102-1131`; `tests/unit_test/pipeline/test_coordinator.py:1544-1569`). One eviction is not guaranteed to restore the budget.

Step 6 uses the running/finished unit test's 10 B cap, so its constructed 12 B queue is already over the limit. The coordinator fails the whole stream rather than dropping selected chunks, because dropping selected chunks would corrupt its audio. On a cap failure, a still-owned running request enters the failure completion path, broadcasts abort and sends the failure to its stream queue. If generation already finished, no running generation remains to abort (`sglang_omni/pipeline/coordinator.py:940-967,1027-1029,1098-1144`; `tests/unit_test/pipeline/test_coordinator.py:1465-1505`). Terminal waveforms use the same accounting (`tests/unit_test/pipeline/test_coordinator.py:1691-1715`), and a terminal waveform that exceeds the cap can fail its own stream (`tests/unit_test/pipeline/test_coordinator.py:1738-1762`).

Step 7 follows the speech-serving failure envelopes documented in `docs/cookbook/qwen3_tts/tuning.md:24-28`. SSE and speech WebSocket readers receive an error event containing `The request queue is full.`; raw PCM has no event envelope and closes early. The SSE implementation emits an error frame and marks `partial_audio=true, action=discard` when audio already went out (`sglang_omni/serve/speech_streaming.py:202-228`). This is not a new pre-response HTTP 503, and this cap does not refuse new requests.

Step 8 separates two close cases. With no cap failure, 12 B remains unread; closing aborts work only while its coordinator work record remains, then removes the stream queue, future and backlog and releases the ID (`sglang_omni/pipeline/coordinator.py:497-553`; `tests/unit_test/pipeline/test_coordinator.py:1625-1638`; Ch. 3 Step 2). After a cap failure, eviction has already removed all counted audio bytes and drained the audio messages. The error remains in the stream queue and the ID stays reserved until the reader takes the error or closes (`sglang_omni/pipeline/coordinator.py:1116-1144`; `tests/unit_test/pipeline/test_coordinator.py:1491-1505`). The closing question moves to the planned Chapter 15: when two API processes share one public URL, which process receives the next request?
