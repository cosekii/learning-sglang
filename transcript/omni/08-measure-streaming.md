## Prose · Where this chapter's claims come from

Step 1 repeats the PR #1235 measurements already shown in Learning SGLang Ch. 19 Step 5. On H200s with 8 concurrent requests, p90 first sound was 2.697 and 2.663 s with the talker beside one thinker half, against 1.463 and 1.415 s on its own GPU. The means are 2.68 and 1.44 s. Only the talker's placement changed. These are public results, not a local benchmark or a promise for another workload.

Step 2 follows `benchmarks/eval/benchmark_omni_streaming_ttft.py:148-205`. The client starts `time.perf_counter()` before streaming the POST. It assigns TTFT only when a delta contains nonempty `audio.data`; text-only deltas do not stop that timer. It measures total time after the stream finishes and requires at least one audio delta. The displayed 0.0, 1.4 and 3.2 seconds are constructed teaching times, not benchmark output.

Step 3 constructs two arrivals at 1.4 and 2.5 seconds around the 800 ms playback chunk from Learning SGLang Ch. 20 Step 1. Chunk 1 therefore plays until 2.2 seconds, leaving a 0.3-second gap. Separately, `benchmarks/metrics/playback_continuity.py:11-83` implements the shipped TTS continuity metric: each chunk starts at its arrival or the previous chunk's end, whichever is later, using measured chunk durations. It keeps each multi-chunk request's longest underrun, reports the largest of those per-request values, and reports `c50`, `c100` and `c200`, the percentages whose longest underrun is at most 50, 100 and 200 ms. One-chunk requests are N/A and do not enter those rates. The TTFT script in Step 2 only counts audio chunks; it does not calculate this metric. The constructed 0.3-second example is not benchmark output.

Step 4 follows `sglang_omni/profiler/comm_trace.py:14-34`, `sglang_omni/comm/engine.py:376-393` and `sglang_omni/pipeline/stage/runtime.py:665-682`. `SGLANG_OMNI_COMM_TRACE=1` enables JSON log records. `ts_ns` comes from `time.time_ns`; `elapsed_ms` measures the local `perf_counter_ns` interval around this read, not the whole request. Object `obj-17` is a constructed identifier, not a captured log.

Step 5 reads `sglang_omni/relay/cuda_ipc.py:526-581`. `elapsed_ms` measures the host-side wait. When start and done CUDA events exist, `receiver_gpu_wait_copy_ms` reports their GPU-event interval and `host_minus_receiver_gpu_ms` subtracts it from the host wait. The 12, 5 and 7 ms values are constructed to teach the fields. The difference is not, by itself, proof of CPU compute time. Worker queue, worker block and resume fields are optional and appear only on the thread-synchronize path.

Step 6 follows the shipped A/B protocol in `benchmarks/eval/benchmark_omni_streaming_ttft.py:11-25,59-69,126-145,224-283,316-340`. Baseline uses `--no-enable-partial-start`; treatment uses `--enable-partial-start --partial-start-min-chunks 5`. Both labels use the same 7-word short prompt and 34-word medium prompt, seed 1000 for sampling choices, a generated-token cap of 256 and the same talker sampling settings. Warmups fill caches and complete first-run setup before the two warmups per prompt are excluded. Five measured repeats per prompt give four warmups and ten kept runs per label. This step shows protocol only and claims no winning result.

Step 7 follows the aggregate calculations at `benchmarks/eval/benchmark_omni_streaming_ttft.py:245-296`: mean, minimum, maximum and population standard deviation. The five values 1.3, 1.4, 1.3, 1.4 and 2.6 seconds are constructed. They sum to 8.0 seconds, so the mean is 1.60 seconds; the population stdev is about 0.502. These values teach why per-run results and spread belong beside the mean. They are not partial-start measurements.

Step 8 combines the benchmark's per-run records and condition fields with the runbook's fixed conditions and completeness checks (`benchmarks/eval/benchmark_omni_streaming_ttft.py:354-365`; `docs/developer_reference/omni_offline_bench.md:310-379,435-443`). A comparison keeps the input and output lengths, hardware, configuration and warmup visible, checks failed requests, word errors and voice match as applicable, and reports spread as well as the mean. `Awesome-ML-SYS-Tutorial/sglang/sglang-omni/batch.md:221-235` gives the same boundary: performance numbers do not replace quality checks, and repeated requests within one run are not independent experiment repeats. The next chapter is `09-ar-stage-optimization`; this chapter does not claim that a cache, asynchronous decode or a CUDA graph already helps.
