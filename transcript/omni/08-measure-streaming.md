## Prose · Where this chapter's claims come from

Step 1 repeats the PR #1235 measurements already shown in Learning SGLang Ch. 19 Step 5. On H200s with 8 concurrent requests, p90 first sound was 2.697 and 2.663 s with the talker beside one thinker half, against 1.463 and 1.415 s on its own GPU. The means are 2.68 and 1.44 s. Only the talker's placement changed. These are public results, not a local benchmark or a promise for another workload.

Step 2 follows `benchmarks/eval/benchmark_omni_streaming_ttft.py:148-205`. The client starts `time.perf_counter()` before streaming the POST. It assigns TTFT only when a delta contains nonempty `audio.data`; text-only deltas do not stop that timer. It measures total time after the stream finishes and requires at least one audio delta. The displayed 0.0, 1.4 and 3.2 seconds are constructed teaching times, not benchmark output.

Step 3 constructs two arrivals at 1.4 and 2.5 seconds around the 800 ms playback chunk from Learning SGLang Ch. 20 Step 1. Chunk 1 therefore plays until 2.2 seconds, leaving a 0.3-second gap. The current TTFT script counts audio chunks but does not store their individual arrival times or calculate stalls. This step shows the additional per-chunk timing arithmetic; it does not claim a shipped stall metric or a measured 0.3-second result.

Step 4 follows `sglang_omni/profiler/comm_trace.py:14-34`, `sglang_omni/comm/engine.py:327-393` and `sglang_omni/pipeline/stage/runtime.py:665-682`. `SGLANG_OMNI_COMM_TRACE=1` enables JSON log records. `ts_ns` comes from `time.time_ns`; an `elapsed_ms` field comes from a local `perf_counter_ns` interval around the named operation, such as enqueueing or reading, not the whole request. Request A, chunk 7 and object `obj-17` are constructed identifiers, not captured logs.

Step 5 reads `sglang_omni/relay/cuda_ipc.py:526-581`. `elapsed_ms` measures the host-side wait. When start and done CUDA events exist, `receiver_gpu_wait_copy_ms` reports their GPU-event interval and `host_minus_receiver_gpu_ms` subtracts it from the host wait. The 12, 5 and 7 ms values are constructed to teach the fields. The difference is not, by itself, proof of CPU compute time. Worker queue, worker block and resume fields are optional and appear only on the thread-synchronize path.

Step 6 follows the shipped A/B protocol in `benchmarks/eval/benchmark_omni_streaming_ttft.py:11-25,59-69,126-145,224-283,316-340`. Baseline uses `--no-enable-partial-start`; treatment uses `--enable-partial-start --partial-start-min-chunks 5`. Both labels use the same short and medium prompts, seed 1000, maximum 256 tokens and talker sampling settings. Each prompt gets two excluded warmups and five measured repeats, giving four warmups and ten kept runs per label. This step shows protocol only and claims no winning result.

Code references in this chapter pin to `sglang-omni origin/main@3a31bd72e034db37ce5115a03dd4827b449683be`. Each public measurement keeps its original setup. The benchmark is read from source, not executed.
