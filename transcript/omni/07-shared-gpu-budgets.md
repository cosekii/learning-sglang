## Prose · Where this chapter's claims come from

Step 1 uses a constructed 80-GiB card, not a memory measurement. It places 60 GiB of weights on the card, leaving 20 GiB for the KV budget. The two fractions are 60 ÷ 80 = 75% and 20 ÷ 80 = 25%, so they account for the whole constructed card. This example isolates the arithmetic; it does not claim that a real model has these sizes or that a live free-memory reading is a KV allocation.

Step 2 uses the paired unit-test cases in `test_placement.py`, not B200 measurements. The stage first converts each fraction into bytes, then adds the reserve, forms the combined declaration and compares it with the physical limit. On a teaching 80-GiB card, a 0.50 fraction plus a 20-GiB total reserve declares 60 GiB and passes. A 0.90 fraction plus the same reserve declares 92 GiB and fails by 12 GiB. The reserve denotes the stage footprint, including its two-GiB KV pool, so that pool is not added to the same reserve again. Placement separately checks that summed KV pools alone fit.

Step 3 constructs a two-GiB `engine.kv_cache_bytes` scope. The stage factory must construct exactly one SGLang engine that consumes it. Zero consumers leave an unconsumed budget and raise on scope exit; a second consumer raises instead of sizing two full pools from one declaration. The two-GiB value is illustrative.

Step 4 compares two symbolic checkpoint copies with the shipped MOSS same-GPU DP2 weight-sharing example. With `weight_share=on`, the leader exports weights over CUDA IPC and one follower aliases them, while both replicas retain private KV pools, graphs and request state. Each engine declares `max_total_tokens=30000`, so the two private pools have a combined token cap of 60,000. The W boxes are symbolic and make no byte-saving claim.

Step 5 constructs two eligible, single-GPU, non-TP processes on one physical GPU. `mps=auto` enables one pipeline-owned MPS lease for their physical UUID and verifies both named clients. TP processes are ineligible; multi-physical-GPU placements are rejected. A lease is an ownership record, not a memory allocation, and MPS does not change the earlier budget arithmetic.

Step 6 follows AuK serial offload through conditioning, DiT and VAE. One request owns the path, one component is resident during each transition and the other two sleep. The old component sleeps before the next wakes. Ownership ends only after terminal-result delivery, failure or cancellation; another request can then begin. Offload requires one CUDA GPU, one process replica, `tp_size=1` and batch size one. Component sizes differ, so two sleeping boxes do not prove a two-thirds byte saving.
