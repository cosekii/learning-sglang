## Prose · Where this chapter's claims come from

Step 1 counts the shipped startup shapes. Text-only mode defines six stages in one `pipeline` process on one GPU and still accepts text, image, audio or video input (`sglang_omni/models/qwen3_omni/config.py:136-147,257-265,412-422`; `docs/cookbook/qwen3_omni.md:46-57,206-208`). Default speech defines seven stages, each in its own process: image encoder, audio encoder, thinker and code2wav use GPU 0, talker uses GPU 1, and preprocessing and decode are CPU stages (`config.py:268-318,425-452`). These counts exclude the HTTP server and coordinator.

Step 2 separates server mode from requested output. Both modes accept multimodal input (`docs/cookbook/qwen3_omni.md:206-208`). Text-only joins active input branches at `mm_aggregate`; the speech thinker merges them itself (`sglang_omni/models/qwen3_omni/config.py:136-189,212-254`). A speech request with `modalities: ["text"]` terminates at decode and excludes talker; adding `"audio"` also terminates at code2wav (`sglang_omni/models/qwen3_omni/request_builders.py:200-261`; `tests/unit_test/qwen3_omni/test_pipeline.py:898-930,1935-1947`). The public default is text output, and a text-only server returns no audio when audio is requested (`docs/basic_usage/omni.md:22,154-170,199-200,359,386-395`).

Step 3 preserves the public PR #1235 topology from Chapter 8 Step 1: H200s, thinker TP2 across GPUs 4 and 5, and code2wav with the thinker process on GPU 5. On the shared GPU, a ready talker step waits for the current thinker turn before it runs. At concurrency eight, moving only the talker from thinker GPU 4 to its own GPU 2 reduced mean-of-two p90 first audio from 2.680 to 1.439 seconds, about 46%. The exact raw values come from PR #1235; the pinned tuning guide publishes rounded ranges and a separate single-stream result where isolation improved first audio but increased end-to-end latency by 14–17% (`docs/cookbook/qwen3_omni/tuning.md:52-69`). That source gives only the relative single-stream range, not the two absolute total times. Step 10's TP1 plan is a different topology and needs its own comparison.

Step 4 starts with the H100 BF16 colocated config's declared `2 + 2 + 78 + 10 + 2 = 94%` (`examples/configs/qwen3_omni_colocated_h100_bf16.yaml:1-20`). The thinker factory defaults `encoder_mem_reserve` to 0.05. With no explicit `thinker.engine.mem_fraction_static`, it subtracts five whole-card percentage points before deriving the AR budget, giving `0.78 − 0.05 = 0.73`; this reserve is headroom for encoder work outside thinker AR, not the separate 2% + 2% encoder stage declarations (`sglang_omni/models/qwen3_omni/stages.py:155-215,1230-1243,1358-1373`; `sglang_omni/scheduling/sglang_backend/server_args_builder.py:176-210`). A matching explicit fraction skips the reserve, a conflicting fraction is rejected, and a byte-sized KV declaration skips the fraction adjustment (`tests/unit_test/qwen3_omni/test_sglang_ar_budget.py:491-511,540-562`; `stages.py:1384-1402`). Colocation requires one common GPU, TP 1, no in-worker process replicas and a declared budget for every GPU stage (`sglang_omni/models/qwen3_omni/placement.py:55-60,84-108,122-187`).

Step 5 compares two constructed video-test lower bounds on one 0–90 GB scale. Both share 82.6 GB before KV: 61.0 GB of thinker weights, 4.0 GB of video reserve, 4.8 GB for other stages, 4.8 GB held back and 8.0 GB for the talker. The previous Learning SGLang Ch. 19 Step 4 tutorial snapshot uses `32,768 × 96 KB = 3.145728 GB` of KV, giving 85.745728 GB before the move and 77.745728 GB after it. PR #2735 makes the checkpoint context the default thinker limit; the Instruct checkpoint's 65,536 tokens give `65,536 × 96 KB = 6.291456 GB`, 88.891456 GB before the move and 80.891456 GB after it. The current default therefore remains 0.891456 GB over the 80 GB card after the talker moves. These are configuration arithmetic and validation rules, not measured occupancy.

Step 6 compares BF16, FP8 and INT4. The compatibility matrix permits each precision for text-only output, separate thinker/talker cards at TP1 or TP2, or one shared card at TP1 (`docs/cookbook/qwen3_omni.md:25-44`). BF16 is the default. The FP8 checkpoint supplies precision config for thinker and talker AR; `--dtype fp8` is not the selection mechanism (`docs/cookbook/qwen3_omni.md:153-169`; `sglang_omni/models/weight_loader.py:19-42`). The INT4 checkpoint quantizes the thinker transformer layers with 4-bit quantization and group size 128, while talker AR and code2wav remain BF16 (`docs/cookbook/qwen3_omni.md:181-195`). The cookbook explicitly names code2wav's BF16 precision for INT4. The implementation also defaults omitted code2wav `dtype` to BF16 for the other rows (`sglang_omni/models/qwen3_omni/components/code2wav_scheduler.py:146-154,1230-1260`; `sglang_omni/models/weight_loader.py:19-26`). The approximately 60, 40 and 30 GB values and their 50%/25% reductions appear in a single-GPU H100/H20 section, but their exact quantity—weights alone, loaded stages, runtime peak or whole-server footprint—is unstated. They are not measured per-card budgets or local footprint, latency or quality measurements (`docs/cookbook/qwen3_omni.md:196-200`).

Step 7 applies the shipped replica2 config. Both topologies keep `image_encoder` and `audio_encoder` with the thinker on GPU 0; default speech also keeps code2wav there. The thinker defaults to 64 running requests; each talker engine defaults to 32 (`sglang_omni/models/qwen3_omni/stages.py:1276-1286`; `sglang_omni/models/qwen3_omni/config.py:37,454-496`). The config places talker/code2wav replica 0 on GPU 1 and replica 1 on GPU 2, with `12.3% + 1.4% = 13.7%` declared per replica GPU (`examples/configs/qwen3_omni_speech_replica2.yaml:1-42`). Default pairing keeps each codec stream on one GPU; a custom policy need not preserve it.

Step 8 derives codec-state capacity from Step 7's two talkers. Each code2wav process computes `codec_state_slots = 32 × 2 = 64` for its own independent pool, covering the streams the talkers can run (`sglang_omni/models/qwen3_omni/config.py:468-476`; `tests/unit_test/qwen3_omni/test_colocation_config.py:177-203`; `sglang_omni/models/qwen3_omni/components/code2wav_scheduler.py:1284-1299`). A full pool falls back to stateless left-context decoding instead of rejecting the stream (`code2wav_scheduler.py:296-314`). This is per-process configuration capacity, not one deployment-wide pool or a throughput measurement.

Step 9 separates health from performance. Readiness sends a small image, one second of audio and a short instruction, with at most eight new tokens per token-generating stage (`docs/cookbook/qwen3_omni.md:202-204`; `sglang_omni/models/qwen3_omni/config.py:328-366`). Video, large batches and other input sizes may still warm later (`docs/basic_usage/omni.md:345-349`). A comparison first resolves the config, then holds input, sampling, hardware and warmup fixed while changing one deployment choice (`docs/basic_usage/process_topology.md:52-53`; Ch. 8 Steps 6 and 8). The documented sweep `--concurrencies 1,16 --repeats 5 --generate-only` gives ten measured runs per arm; `c` means concurrency, the requests running together. Per-run raw results, failures and memory stay in the comparison. The benchmark client measures first-audio and total-request wall time, separate from listener playback stalls and the Ch. 12 stop/response clocks (`benchmarks/metrics/performance.py:27-31`; `benchmarks/eval/benchmark_omni_seedtts.py:371,398,408`). The shipped summary provides first-audio median, p95 and p99; Step 10's p90 is a custom reduction of non-null `per_request.audio_ttfp_s` values (`benchmarks/metrics/performance.py:219-223,480`). The benchmark notes that p99 needs at least 100 measured requests in the sample, not merely a particular number of runs (`benchmarks/README.md:249-288`; `benchmarks/eval/benchmark_omni_seedtts.py:504-505,552-561`). Generate-only does not transcribe audio or compute WER or speaker similarity, so Ch. 8 Step 8's quality checks run separately (`benchmarks/README.md:215-220,659`). The listener's stall clock uses the playback deadline from Learning SGLang Ch. 20 Steps 1–3: ten 80 ms frames make one 800 ms chunk, and the next chunk must be ready before the current chunk ends. The pending-to-ready animation is a constructed timing example, not a compute-time measurement.

Step 10 constructs one TP1 starting hypothesis for eight concurrent spoken requests: default BF16 speech, text and audio output, input encoders, thinker and code2wav on GPU 0, and talker alone on GPU 1. This is motivated by Step 3's different H200/TP2 evidence. The local A/B holds that two-GPU layout fixed and compares only the default BF16 checkpoint with the native FP8 checkpoint, changing checkpoint precision. INT4 needs separate memory and quality evidence and is not measured in this first comparison. With only `c = 8`, five repeats give five runs per arm. Eight is below the 32-request talker cap, leaving `32 − 8 = 24` requests of headroom, so the example does not add a replica before measuring a queue. The evidence procedure uses target concurrency 8 and keeps first-audio median and custom p90, total time, stalls, failures, memory, output quality and per-run raw results; Ch. 8 Step 8 names the completion, word-error and voice-match checks outside the generate-only sweep, and interruption tests also keep the Ch. 12 listener clocks.  The closing cue is `client ← queued audio (API process)`, where queued audio is unread sound waiting for the client. The closing question opens the serving part: after deployment, what happens when a client reads too slowly?
