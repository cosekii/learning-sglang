## Prose · Where this chapter's claims come from

Step 1 uses a constructed 2.5-second reply. The delivery ledger reaches 2.0 seconds and the client reports 1.2 seconds played, leaving 800 ms delivered but unplayed. `DeliveryPacer.sent()` counts audio handed to the client; it does not observe acoustic playback (`sglang_omni/pipeline/session_delivery.py:112-128`). The default `max_output_lead_ms` is 1,000 ms (`sglang_omni/proto/session.py:19-28`), so the constructed 800 ms interval fits within that delivery horizon. Later steps reuse these exact positions.

Step 2 maps alternatives rather than stages in one pipeline. Turn-based and native full-duplex recall Learning SGLang Ch. 21 Steps 1–2. The new streaming-cascade route sends audio through ASR, a chat-completion LLM and sentence-wise TTS (`docs/developer_reference/full_duplex_guide.md:1582-1591,1622-1645`). MiniCPM-o consumes 1,000 ms units and makes model-led listen/speak decisions (`docs/developer_reference/full_duplex_guide.md:391-397`). These are different systems, so the chapter does not treat their measurements as an isolated architecture benchmark.

Step 3 follows the Session graph's turn-named interrupt. It drops matching chunks from the target inbox, unsent appends, the delivery pacer and unread client output, while preserving client input (`sglang_omni/pipeline/session_graph.py:108-173`). A preempting interrupt also names already submitted appends so their owners abort them (`session_graph.py:175-210`). Later output from an interrupted reply remains fenced (`session_graph.py:581-619`; `sglang_omni/pipeline/sessions.py:167-210`). `reply.delivered` reports the connection's delivered duration (`session_graph.py:212-232`; `session_delivery.py:112-128`), not the listener's acoustic position.
